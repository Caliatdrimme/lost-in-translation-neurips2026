Lost in Translation, Found in Interdisciplinarity

A playful evening for anyone curious about language and translation, human and artificial alike.

Tuesday 8 December 2026, 7:00 to 9:00 PM Rooms C2.2 and C2.3, ICC Sydney, NeurIPS 2026

?

What do schadenfreude, saudade, and komorebi have in common?

Show the answer

Each names a feeling or moment that English has no tidy single word for:

  • Schadenfreude (German): pleasure at someone else's misfortune. English just borrowed it.
  • Saudade (Portuguese): a bittersweet longing for someone or something absent.
  • Komorebi (Japanese): sunlight filtering through the leaves of trees.
?

Which Kafka did you mean?

Show the answer

It depends on your field. To a literature person, Kafka is the author of The Metamorphosis. To an engineer, it is the Apache streaming service that moves data around the cloud, named after him: its creator Jay Kreps has said he chose the name because the system is optimized for writing and he liked the author.

Same word, two fields, and a whole conversation can go sideways before anyone notices. ML has plenty more: transformer, kernel, token, attention, python.

Whatever your field, you've probably explained your work to someone outside it and watched something get lost along the way. Ever had an LLM misread you completely because of a quirk of language? Ever tried to translate a joke, an idiom, or a feeling between two languages you speak, and found it wouldn't cross over?

Often it is not a missing word but a shared one. The same term can mean two different things in two fields, and nobody notices until the conversation goes sideways.

This evening is for playing with those gaps, and for meeting people on the other side of them.

What happens

You team up with a few neighbors for a series of short, playful challenges that stretch how you explain, question, and think across fields. No expertise needed, and no one plays alone.

  • Explain

    Make your work land with a new audience.

  • Question

    Spot what each field takes for granted.

  • Debate

    Find where fields disagree, and why.

  • Translate

    Watch familiar words change meaning.

  • Build

    Solve something none of you could alone.

Then, bingo

Take your card and go find people you'd never have crossed paths with otherwise. You'll leave with sharper communication instincts, fresh ways of thinking across fields, and a handful of new contacts.

The story board

All evening, anyone can post a short story to the board: an LLM that misunderstood you, a translation that went wrong, a time two fields used the same word for different things. Everyone can vote for their favorites. At the end we read the most-loved ones aloud and give their authors a shoutout. We will show you how on the night, and you can start thinking of yours now.

Who it's for

Linguists, translators, MT and ML folks, and anyone with expertise in another area who wants to talk to people outside it. If you'd like to get better at explaining your work, thinking across fields, or just meeting curious people, you're in the right room.

Who can come

Anyone with a NeurIPS 2026 main conference or tutorial registration. There is no separate sign-up for this event, so just show up.
